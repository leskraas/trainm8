Decisions

  1. Who holds the copy? The plan (Plan Outline), one copy per pick, shared by the plan's own days where the write shares it.
  2. How is a held copy marked? A nullable Workout.heldByOutlineId, FK to PlanOutline with onDelete: Cascade.
  3. When does a copy go? With the plan by cascade. When a day, pattern, track or lifting rewrite drops the last day holding it, a plan-scoped sweep removes it in the same transaction. A day choice in Avansert does not sweep, so its undo still works.
  4. What does the migration do? For each plan, one copy of each Workout its days share with a session, a Stock Workout or another plan. A Workout only that plan's days hold gets marked held, with no copy.
  5. Do stock lifting slots get a copy too? Yes, a held copy with copiedFromId pointing at the Stock Workout, so the Citation still reads through the copy.
  6. Critic. What happens to a day when its Workout is deleted? The delete fails. WeekPatternDay.workoutId changes from SetNull to NoAction in the same migration, so deleting a plan, an Event or an account cascades cleanly in any order, and deleting a held Workout that a day still uses fails loudly instead of nulling it.
  7. Critic. Which Workout ids does a pattern write accept? Only rows held by this plan. A session's Workout or a Stock Workout is copied in on the way. This replaces the "owned or stock" guard in track-days checkedRestore and in lifting-days, so an undo payload issued before the deploy cannot tie a day to a session again.
  8. Critic. When does a day choice's orphaned copy go? At the next write to the plan. Every write sweeps held rows that no day uses, except the ones its own undo still names. At most one write's rows wait, and nothing else lingers until the plan is deleted.
  9. Critic. How is the SQL deep copy kept faithful? An integration test runs the migration on fixture plans and compares the next stamped week before and after (AC5). The copy goes through every column workoutCopySelect carries.

Fit. This follows ADR 0051's fork-on-write, with copiedFromId as provenance, so slice 2 (saved and stock Workouts in the picker) is the same copy-on-pick. It adds a second "whose is this" axis on Workout, so every future "my Workouts" reader has to skip held rows. Put that filter in one shared where fragment rather than repeating it in each reader. The plan sharing one copy across its days differs from the Scope's "its own copy". The criteria do not ask for a copy per day, and a copy per day would break unitsOf.

Method

Logic and state question → Matt's prototype, logic branch (.claude/skills/prototype/LOGIC.md): one pure module (PatternWorkouts) and a thin page on top. The acceptance criteria are rendered as live checks. A Playwright probe ran every walkthrough under all three models on both data sets: Today fails AC1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 as the issue says, Copy per day splits Simple Activities, and Plan holds its copies passes everything.

Code read on origin/main 6ec5b58: track-days.server.ts (unitsOf groups a Simple Activity by workoutId; replaceUnitRows deletes and rewrites rows; checkedRestore restores by workoutId), lifting-days.server.ts (writeStrengthRows deletes and rewrites rows, and every slot shares one id), strength-offer.server.ts, authoring.server.ts (addWeekPatternDay, starter pattern), first-run/plan.server.ts, start-strength.server.ts (lifting falls back to the Stock Workout), account-deletion.server.ts, community.server.ts (listOwnPublishableWorkouts lists every owned athlete Workout), training.server.ts (getAuthoredWorkouts reads through authored sessions), and workout.server.ts (deleteOrphanedLineage counts patternDays).

Rejected directions Rejected

  • Copy per day row (the issue's Scope, read literally): a 3-day Simple Activity becomes 3 units in Avansert. Lifting slots get N identical copies. Delete-and-rewrite has to copy before deleting, and an undo restores ids that have already been swept.
  • Mark copies by inference ("has pattern days and no sessions"): this cannot tell a held copy from a legitimate row, and the Catalogue's "your sessions" list would show the copies.
  • SQLite AFTER DELETE trigger for cleanup: it fires in the middle of a rewrite and breaks undo, and Prisma cannot see it.
  • FK from the Workout to the day, with cascade: one owner per row, which rules out sharing. It also cascades away the copy during delete-and-rewrite.

Designer's note

The designer's pick is Plan holds its copies, the same variant I recommend. The designer's reason: all 8 checks pass live, and a Simple Activity stays one unit. My answer: on the real schema, AC6 fails until the day FK is NoAction. The designer's draft also has checkedRestore accept held rows in addition to owned and stock rows. I would accept only held rows, because otherwise the tie can come back (Decision 7).
